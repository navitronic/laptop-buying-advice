Year 7 laptop guide · 2027

Buying outside the school program?

You do not need a powerful or expensive laptop for normal schoolwork. Focus on battery life, durability, weight and enough memory to last a few years.

Buying elsewhere is allowed. The school’s Digital Technology Policy permits a family-supplied device when it meets the published specifications. Only CentreCom devices receive full hardware and software troubleshooting from the school.

The simple recommendation

Look for these specifications

Device type

Start here

Choose a laptop or notebook

The school says Year 7 students are expected to have a notebook. A conventional laptop is a safer choice than a tablet for typing, compatibility and everyday classroom use.

Battery

Most important

10 hours or more advertised

The school does not provide charging during the day. Its minimum is 8 hours, but real battery life is often lower than advertised.

Memory

Important

16 GB preferred; 8 GB minimum

This keeps browsers, Teams, OneNote and documents running smoothly. Choose 16 GB if the laptop should last into the senior years or run creative software. Avoid 4 GB.

Storage

Important

256 GB SSD or larger

This is enough for schoolwork when using cloud storage. A 512 GB SSD is useful, but not necessary.

Size and weight

Important

13–14 inch screen; under 1.5 kg

This is large enough for schoolwork without adding too much weight to a school bag.

Processor

Keep it sensible

Intel Core i3 12th Gen or AMD Ryzen 3 7000 series

This is the school’s published minimum processor class; newer or faster versions are also suitable. Do not confuse an entry-level Intel N-series chip with a Core i3. An i7, Ryzen 7 or gaming graphics will add cost without helping normal schoolwork.

Creative software

Only if needed

Allow more power for Adobe applications

The school warns that two of its cheaper 2027 options are not suitable for Adobe products. For digital art, design or media, prefer 16 GB of memory and a Core i5, Ryzen 5 or better, then check the software’s requirements before buying.

Operating system

Safest choice

Windows 11

Windows is the simplest choice for matching the school setup. School documents conflict between Windows 10 and Windows 11, so confirm that the exact laptop includes Windows 11 before buying. Microsoft 365 is provided to students by the school.

Optional extra

A pen is probably not necessary

A touchscreen is common on many suitable laptops and can be convenient, but it should not be a deciding factor. Normal schoolwork does not depend on one.

Treat pen support as a separate extra. The school policy recommends a stylus only for touchscreen or 2-in-1 devices. It is worth paying for mainly if your child plans to do digital art or strongly prefers handwritten notes. Otherwise, prioritise battery life, build quality or warranty.

Often overlooked

Practical features matter more

Durability

Look for strong hinges, a firm case and a spill-resistant keyboard. The laptop will travel to and from school every day.

Warranty and insurance

Families are responsible for damage and repairs. Compare warranty length, battery coverage, onsite repairs, insurance excess and claim limits—not only the premium.

Keyboard and webcam

Try the keyboard if possible. Check that the webcam and microphone are adequate for online classes and group work.

Protective case

Buy a well-fitted protective case. The school expects the device to be carried inside its case and school bag.

School minimums

Requirements for a family-supplied device

The school’s Digital Technology Policy gives more detailed requirements for devices purchased outside the CentreCom program.

  • Intel Core i3 12th Gen, AMD Ryzen 3 7000 series or better
  • At least 8 GB RAM and a 256 GB SSD
  • At least a 13.3-inch screen
  • Windows 11 or an equivalent operating system
  • At least 8 hours advertised battery life
  • Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.2 or newer
  • Headphones and a protective carry case or sleeve

Sources: Digital Technology Policy, Digital Device Program 2027 , 2027 BYOD Parent Information Sheet, and Booklists and digital devices.

Final check

Before you buy

  • Confirm the model is suitable with the school.
  • Choose a laptop or notebook rather than a tablet.
  • Check that Windows 11 is included.
  • Check the exact processor, RAM and storage configuration.
  • Check for Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.2.
  • Check Adobe compatibility if creative software will be used.
  • Read independent battery-life reviews.
  • Check the laptop weighs less than 1.5 kg.
  • Include a protective case and headphones in the budget.
  • Compare warranty, battery and accidental-damage coverage.
  • Clearly label the device with the student’s name and class.
