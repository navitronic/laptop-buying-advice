Year 7 laptop guide · 2027

Buying outside the school program?

You do not need a powerful or expensive laptop for normal schoolwork. Focus on battery life, durability, weight and enough memory to last a few years.

Before buying: confirm with Northcote High School that a device bought outside its preferred CentreCom program can connect to school services and meet classroom expectations.

The simple recommendation

Look for these specifications

Device type

Start here

Choose a laptop or notebook

The school says Year 7 students are expected to have a notebook. A conventional laptop is a safer choice than a tablet for typing, compatibility and everyday classroom use.

Battery

Most important

10 hours or more advertised

The school does not provide charging during the day. Its minimum is 8 hours, but real battery life is often lower than advertised.

Memory

Important

16 GB preferred; 8 GB minimum

This keeps browsers, Teams, OneNote and documents running smoothly. Avoid 4 GB.

Storage

Important

256 GB SSD or larger

This is enough for schoolwork when using cloud storage. A 512 GB SSD is useful, but not necessary.

Size and weight

Important

13–14 inch screen; under 1.5 kg

This is large enough for schoolwork without adding too much weight to a school bag.

Processor

Keep it sensible

Recent Intel Core i3/i5 or AMD Ryzen 3/5

These are more than adequate. An i7, Ryzen 7 or dedicated gaming graphics will add cost without helping normal schoolwork.

Creative software

Only if needed

Allow more power for Adobe applications

The school warns that two of its cheaper 2027 options are not suitable for Adobe products. For digital art, design or media, prefer 16 GB of memory and a Core i5, Ryzen 5 or better, then check the software’s requirements before buying.

Operating system

Safest choice

Windows 11

Windows is the simplest choice for matching the school setup. School documents conflict between Windows 10 and Windows 11, so confirm that the exact laptop includes Windows 11 before buying. Microsoft 365 is provided to students by the school.

Optional extra

A pen is probably not necessary

A touchscreen is common on many suitable laptops and can be convenient, but it should not be a deciding factor. Normal schoolwork does not depend on one.

Treat pen support as a separate extra. It is worth paying for mainly if your child plans to do digital art or strongly prefers handwritten notes. Otherwise, prioritise battery life, build quality or warranty.

Often overlooked

Practical features matter more

Durability

Look for strong hinges, a firm case and a spill-resistant keyboard. The laptop will travel to and from school every day.

Warranty and insurance

Families are responsible for damage and repairs. A longer warranty or accidental-damage cover may be worth the cost.

Keyboard and webcam

Try the keyboard if possible. Check that the webcam and microphone are adequate for online classes and group work.

Protective case

Buy a well-fitted protective case. The school expects the device to be carried inside its case and school bag.

School minimums

What the 2027 handbook requires

The handbook lists these general minimum specifications for a 1:1 device. These are minimums, not necessarily the best buying target.

  • At least a 9.7-inch screen
  • Wireless internet capability
  • Advertised battery life of 8 hours
  • Word processing, spreadsheet and presentation software
  • A PDF reader

Sources: Digital Device Program 2027 , 2027 BYOD Parent Information Sheet, and Booklists and digital devices.

Final check

Before you buy

  • Confirm the model is suitable with the school.
  • Choose a laptop or notebook rather than a tablet.
  • Check that Windows 11 is included.
  • Check the exact RAM and storage configuration.
  • Check Adobe compatibility if creative software will be used.
  • Read independent battery-life reviews.
  • Check the laptop weighs less than 1.5 kg.
  • Include a protective case in the budget.
  • Compare warranty and accidental-damage options.
