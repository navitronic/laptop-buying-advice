Start here

What should I buy?

For most Year 7 students, I would look for:

  • Windows 11
  • 16 GB RAM
  • 256–512 GB SSD
  • Recent Intel Core i5, Core 5 or AMD Ryzen 5
  • 13.3–14-inch screen, under 1.5 kg
  • 10+ hours advertised battery life
  • Durable construction and a three-year warranty

16 GB RAM is the priority. A touchscreen is convenient but optional; a pen mainly matters for digital art or handwritten notes.

School compatibility

Can I buy outside CentreCom?

The school’s October 2025 policy allows families to supply their own device when it meets the published specifications. CentreCom devices are preferred and receive additional troubleshooting support.

Which document says what?

These are not simply old and new versions of the same policy. The 2025 document is a school-wide Digital Technology Policy. The 2027 documents describe the device program and the supplier’s actual models.

Source Scope Minimums or guidance
October 2025 Digital Technology Policy Describes requirements for personal BYOD devices. Core i3 12th Gen or Ryzen 3 7000 series, 8 GB RAM, 256 GB SSD, 13.3-inch screen, Windows 11, 8-hour battery, Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.2.

What this means for an independent Year 7 purchase

The detailed minimums quoted in this guide come from the October 2025 Digital Technology Policy. The 2027 handbook does not publish an alternative set of minimums for independently purchased Year 7 laptops—it publishes the CentreCom model list instead. Some of those models do not match the 2025 policy figures.

NHS has not clearly explained which standard it will apply to an independent Year 7 device. Consider asking for written confirmation at info@nhs.vic.edu.au before purchasing.

Part 1

What affects the price of a laptop?

Laptop prices are determined by more than speed. Build quality, weight, battery life and support can matter more to a student than an expensive processor.

Processor (CPU)

Speed

The processor affects how quickly apps respond and how well the laptop handles demanding work. Intel Core and AMD Ryzen chips are organised into performance levels. Entry-level Intel N-series chips are cheaper but are not the same class as a Core i3.

Paying for a faster processor helps with video editing, design and other intensive software. It makes little difference to documents, web browsing and Microsoft Teams once the basic requirement is met.

Intel/AMD versus ARM

Compatibility and battery

Most Windows laptops use Intel or AMD processors with the x86-64 architecture. They provide the broadest compatibility with Windows software, accessories and device drivers. Windows ARM laptops usually use Qualcomm Snapdragon processors and can offer excellent battery life, low heat and quiet operation.

Microsoft 365, Teams and modern browsers run well on ARM. Windows 11 can also translate many older Intel applications, but not every program, driver, VPN, security tool or peripheral will work. Driver compatibility cannot be solved by emulation, so printers and specialist hardware need particular care.

Memory (RAM)

Multitasking

Memory holds the apps, browser tabs and documents currently in use. Once a laptop meets the basic processor and SSD requirements, RAM will usually have the biggest effect on its day-to-day feel. More RAM means fewer slowdowns when several things are open.

8 GB is sufficient for current schoolwork, but leaves little spare capacity and is not future-proof. 16 GB is the clear preference. RAM is often permanently attached and cannot be upgraded later.

Storage (SSD)

File capacity

Storage holds apps and files when the laptop is turned off. An SSD is fast and standard in modern laptops. More storage is useful for photos, videos and large creative projects, but normal school files are small and can also be stored in Microsoft’s cloud services.

Screen

Size and features

Larger screens cost more and add weight. Higher resolution improves sharpness. Better brightness and an anti-glare finish make the screen easier to use in a classroom.

Touchscreens are common and convenient, but not essential. Pen support is a separate feature mainly worth paying for when a child plans to create digital art or prefers handwritten notes.

Graphics (GPU)

Creative and 3D work

Integrated graphics are included with the processor and are enough for ordinary schoolwork. Dedicated graphics increase the price, weight and power use. They are mainly useful for advanced 3D work, video production and gaming.

Build and weight

Daily durability

Thin, light and strong laptops cost more because their materials and hinges are more expensive. Business and education models often add spill-resistant keyboards, stronger cases and easier repairs. These features can be more valuable at school than extra speed.

Battery

Time away from power

Battery life depends on battery size, processor efficiency, screen brightness and workload. Manufacturer claims are useful for comparison, but independent reviews usually provide a more realistic result.

Warranty and support

Risk and convenience

A cheaper consumer laptop may include only a one-year return-to-base warranty. Education and business models may include three-year onsite service, battery coverage and optional accidental-damage insurance. These benefits are part of the price.

Part 2

What does a Year 7 student need?

NHS students use Microsoft 365, Teams, OneNote, Compass, web resources and digital textbooks. The device must travel every day and cannot be charged at school.

Laptop type

School website and 2027 program A conventional laptop or notebook

NHS says Year 7 students are expected to have a notebook. A tablet is less practical for regular typing and may require a separate keyboard.

Processor

October 2025 policy Core i3 12th Gen or Ryzen 3 7000 series

My preference is a recent Core i5, Core 5 or Ryzen 5 for longer life and creative software. Intel or AMD is the safest compatibility choice. ARM can provide excellent battery life, but NHS does not explicitly address it and every required app and accessory should be checked first.

Memory

My recommendation Choose 16 GB if at all possible

RAM has a major effect on everyday responsiveness when Teams, OneNote, documents and browser tabs are open together. 8 GB meets the October 2025 policy and is sufficient today, but it is limited and not a future-proof purchase. Avoid 4 GB.

Storage

October 2025 policy 256 GB SSD

A 256 GB SSD is enough for normal schoolwork with cloud storage. Choose 512 GB for large creative projects or extra breathing room.

Screen and weight

October 2025 policy At least 13.3 inches

My preference is a 13.3–14-inch laptop under 1.5 kg. Note that the separate 2027 Year 7–8 list includes an 11.6-inch CentreCom model, so confirm how the October 2025 policy applies to independent purchases.

Battery

My recommendation 10 hours or more advertised

The October 2025 policy requires at least 8 hours. All three 2027 CentreCom options advertise 10 hours or more, and the school does not offer charging during the day. Aim higher because advertised battery life is normally optimistic.

Operating system

October 2025 policy Windows 11 or equivalent

Windows 11 is the simplest match for the school setup. The policy requires Windows 11, while the 2027 CentreCom brochure advertises Windows 10. Confirm the installed version before purchase.

Wireless

October 2025 policy Wi-Fi 6 and Bluetooth 5.2 or newer

These are explicit requirements in the October 2025 policy.

Touch and pen

My recommendation Touch is optional; a pen is a specialist extra

Neither is needed for ordinary schoolwork. Consider pen support for digital art or handwritten notes, not as a general requirement.

Adobe and creative work

2027 CentreCom information sheet Check software requirements separately

NHS provides Adobe Creative Cloud, but warns that two cheaper 2027 CentreCom options are unsuitable for Adobe products. Prefer 16 GB RAM and a Core i5, Core 5 or Ryzen 5 if creative work matters.

Protection

My recommendation Protective case, three-year warranty and insurance

Families are responsible for physical damage and repairs. Compare battery coverage, onsite service, insurance excess and claim limits.

NHS sources: Digital Technology Policy, Digital Device Program 2027, CentreCom device details, and Booklists and digital devices.

Part 3

How and where to buy

1

Decide how long it should last

Prefer 16 GB RAM even for a three-year purchase. Consider 8 GB only when the budget requires it and accept that it has less headroom and may need replacing sooner. For five or six years, also favour a mid-range processor and a longer warranty.

2

Compare exact model numbers

The same product name can cover several configurations. Confirm the processor generation, RAM, storage, operating system, screen, weight and warranty on the model actually in stock.

3

See it in person

Visit a store and handle the laptop if possible. Check whether the case flexes, the hinges feel solid, the keyboard and trackpad are comfortable, and the screen is easy to read. Pick it up with the charger and imagine carrying it every school day.

4

Read independent reviews

Search the exact model number with “review” and check measured battery life, keyboard quality, noise, screen brightness and repairability.

5

Compare the complete package

Add the case, warranty and accidental-damage cover before comparing prices. A slightly dearer education model may be better value than a cheaper laptop with limited support.

Compare with the school program

Open the NHS CentreCom portal to compare the school-selected devices, warranties and support packages. You can also read my comparison of the three CentreCom options.

Part 4

Due diligence checklists

Before buying

  • Get written NHS confirmation if buying independently.
  • Check the exact processor, model number and architecture.
  • Choose 16 GB RAM; treat 8 GB as a budget compromise.
  • Confirm at least a 256 GB SSD and Windows 11.
  • Check Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.2 and realistic battery life.
  • Handle the laptop and charger in person if possible.
  • Confirm required Adobe or specialist software will run.
  • Understand warranty, repair and insurance terms.

Before the first day

  • Buy a well-fitted protective case and headphones.
  • Label the laptop with the student’s name and class.
  • Install current security and operating-system updates.
  • Set up a reliable cloud or external backup.
  • Connect school accounts and install required software.
  • Confirm the charger remains at home.
  • Charge the device fully overnight.
