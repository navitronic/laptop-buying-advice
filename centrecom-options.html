NHS CentreCom program · 2027

Comparing the school options

The school package offers easier support and education-focused warranty options. The three laptops are very different in price, performance and likely useful life.

Before relying on this comparison

Confirm the final specifications

Prices and specifications below come from the NHS 2027 BYOD Parent Information Sheet and may change. The brochure lists Windows 10 on all three laptops, while the NHS Digital Technology Policy requires Windows 11. Ask CentreCom or the school to confirm in writing that the delivered laptop includes Windows 11.

The October policy specifies a 13.3-inch screen and Core i3-class processor for personal devices. The separate 2027 Year 7–8 handbook recommends the CentreCom list, including models that do not match those figures. These may be approved exceptions, but NHS has not clearly explained how the policy applies to independently purchased Year 7 devices.

Ordering deadline: the brochure says orders should be completed by 18 November 2026 for scheduled delivery. The EliteBook is expected to ship in the week starting 7 December 2026; the ASUS and Fortis in the week starting 18 January 2027. Later orders may not receive a scheduled delivery timeframe.

Budget option

ASUS ExpertBook

$979

A practical school laptop with a strong included warranty. Its main compromise is 8 GB RAM and an entry-level processor.

Intel Core 3 N355 8 GB RAM 256 GB SSD 14-inch non-touch Up to 10 hours

What is good

  • Lowest price of the three school options.
  • Useful 14-inch Full HD anti-glare screen.
  • Spill-resistant keyboard.
  • Three-year onsite warranty, including the battery.
  • Optional accidental-damage cover is comparatively inexpensive: $181.50 for three years, $0 excess and uncapped claims according to the brochure.
  • Enough performance for Microsoft 365 and normal web-based work.

What is not so good

  • 8 GB of RAM is sufficient today but limited and not future-proof.
  • The brochure calls the processor “Intel Core i3-N355”. Intel’s product name is Core 3 N355; it is an entry-level N-series chip, not a conventional Core i3.
  • The brochure says it is unsuitable for Adobe products.
  • No touchscreen or pen, if those features matter to the student.
  • The processor description and Windows version should be confirmed against the school’s published minimums.

My view: this is the most reasonable school option for ordinary Year 7 work. I would ask whether a 16 GB version or memory upgrade is available. If not, treat it as a good lower-cost laptop rather than the best choice for all six years of secondary school.

Convertible option

HP Fortis Flip G1i with Pen

$1,441

A small rugged convertible designed around touch and pen use. Most of the extra cost buys the form factor rather than better performance.

Intel N150 8 GB RAM 256 GB SSD 11.6-inch touch Up to 10 hours

What is good

  • Rugged education-focused construction.
  • Compact and likely easy to carry.
  • Touchscreen, convertible hinge and included pen.
  • Pick- and spill-resistant keyboard.
  • May suit a student committed to handwritten notes or drawing.

What is not so good

  • The Intel N150 is the weakest processor of the three options.
  • 8 GB of RAM gives little future headroom.
  • The 11.6-inch screen is below the October policy’s 13.3-inch figure, although it appears on the separate approved Year 7–8 list.
  • Only a one-year warranty is included.
  • The brochure says it is unsuitable for Adobe products.
  • Extending the warranty with accidental-damage cover costs $407, taking the package to $1,848 before a case or other accessories.

My view: this is difficult to recommend. It costs much more than the ASUS while offering weaker performance and the same limited RAM. It makes sense only when the rugged convertible design and pen are genuinely important. Ask the school how the separate Year 7–8 approval relates to the October policy’s screen and processor figures.

Premium option

HP EliteBook x360 1040 G11

$2,068

A premium business convertible with the memory, storage and processor most likely to remain useful for several years.

Intel Core Ultra 5 16 GB RAM 512 GB SSD 14-inch touch Up to 21 hours

What is good

  • 16 GB of RAM is the right amount to buy.
  • Much stronger processor than the other school options.
  • 512 GB storage provides ample room.
  • 14-inch anti-glare touchscreen and included pen.
  • Backlit spill-resistant keyboard.
  • Three-year onsite warranty.
  • Best chance of remaining useful into the senior years.

What is not so good

  • More than twice the price of the ASUS.
  • More performance than most Year 7 schoolwork requires.
  • A premium device is an expensive item to carry to school daily.
  • The advertised 21-hour battery claim should be checked against an independent review.
  • The brochure’s Windows 10 listing still needs clarification.

My view: this is clearly the best computer, and the only school option with the 16 GB RAM I would prefer. It may be good value for this class of business laptop, but it is still a large amount to spend on a Year 7 device. Compare it with 16 GB retail alternatives before deciding.

Decision guide

Which one would I choose?

Keeping the cost down

ASUS ExpertBook

The most balanced official option, especially with its included three-year warranty. Accept that 8 GB RAM limits its useful life.

Keeping it for many years

HP EliteBook

The strongest long-term specification, provided the price and risk of carrying a premium device are acceptable.

Needing a pen and rugged convertible

HP Fortis—with caution

Choose it only for those specific features, after confirming its policy exceptions and full three-year package cost.

Wanting the best hardware value

Compare outside the program

Look for a 14-inch Windows 11 laptop with 16 GB RAM, a recent Core i5, Core 5 or Ryzen 5, and suitable warranty coverage.

Before ordering

Questions for CentreCom or NHS

  • Will the laptop arrive with Windows 11 installed and activated?
  • Why do some listed models differ from the published BYOD minimums?
  • Can the ASUS be supplied with 16 GB RAM?
  • Is the RAM upgradeable after purchase?
  • What exactly is covered by the battery warranty?
  • What exclusions apply to accidental-damage protection?
  • Who handles repairs and what is the expected turnaround time?
  • Is a loan device available while a CentreCom laptop is repaired?

Source: NHS 2027 BYOD Parent Information Sheet and NHS Digital Technology Policy. Last verified: 9 November 2026.
